================================================================================
TWO-MICROPHONE RECORDINGS  --  what we need, exactly
SIH 2026, problem 26052 (DRDO), AI/ML workstream -- Phase 3
================================================================================

Please read this once fully before you start. It is long because four things can
silently ruin a recording session, and we only find out weeks later when the
data turns out to be unusable. Every "DO NOT" below is something that has
already gone wrong once on this project and cost us a re-record.

Time needed: about 60-75 minutes per session, one person recording, one or two
speaking. You do not need any special equipment beyond two phones.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY WE NEED THIS  (30 seconds of background, worth reading)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Our model currently listens to ONE microphone and tries to work out which parts
are voice and which are gunfire. It is good at it, but there is a hard limit: on
a clip that only contains 5 dB of noise, no amount of cleverness extracts 15 dB
of improvement. The problem statement asks for 15 dB.

The helmet has TWO microphones: one inside, in front of the mouth, and one on
top, which mostly hears the surroundings. If the model can hear the noise ON ITS
OWN from the second mic, it can subtract it instead of guessing. That is the
realistic route to the 15 dB target.

To build that, we need recordings where the SAME moment was captured by a mic at
the mouth AND a mic away from the mouth, at the same time. That is all this is:
two phones recording together.

We cannot fake this. We tried simulating the second microphone in software, and
the result only tells us how good our simulation is, not how good the method is.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT YOU NEED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * TWO phones (any two Android/iPhone). If you can get two of the SAME model,
    that is better, but any two are fine.
  * A third device or a speaker to play noise from (a Bluetooth speaker, a
    laptop, a TV -- anything reasonably loud).
  * A quiet room, and access to 2-3 noisy places (see section 6).
  * Something to prop the phones up: a stack of books, tape, a tripod, a friend's
    steady hand. The phones MUST NOT MOVE during a take.
  * This document.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE FOUR RULES THAT MATTER MOST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If you remember nothing else, remember these. Each one, if broken, makes the
recording worthless for our purpose -- not "slightly worse", worthless.

RULE 1 -- USE A PLAIN VOICE RECORDER APP. NEVER THE VIDEO CAMERA.
    Phone video recording applies its own noise suppression and automatic volume
    control. We measured this: switching from video capture to a plain voice
    recorder recovered 19.2 dB of the frequencies that carry consonants. The
    camera app quietly destroys exactly what we are trying to study.
    Android: "Recorder" / "Voice Recorder" / "Easy Voice Recorder".
    iPhone: "Voice Memos".

RULE 2 -- BLUETOOTH OFF ON BOTH PHONES. NO EARBUDS, NO HEADSET, NO SMARTWATCH.
    A Bluetooth headset microphone in call mode is 30 dB down by 1300 Hz. Every
    consonant cue is simply never recorded. If a Bluetooth device is connected,
    the phone may silently record through IT instead of its own microphone.
    Turn Bluetooth OFF in settings -- not just "disconnect".

RULE 3 -- BOTH PHONES STAY STILL AND KEEP THE SAME POSITIONS FOR A WHOLE TAKE.
    The method works by learning the acoustic path between the two mics. Move a
    phone mid-take and that path changes, and the take is unusable. Prop them up
    and do not touch them until the take ends. Between takes you may move them.

RULE 4 -- CLAP ONCE AT THE START AND ONCE AT THE END OF EVERY TAKE.
    The two phones have independent clocks; they drift apart by a few
    milliseconds per minute. Two sharp claps, one at each end, let us measure and
    correct that drift. Without them we cannot line the two recordings up, and
    everything downstream is meaningless.
    ONE sharp clap. Not applause. Right in between the two phones, so both hear
    it clearly. See the exact procedure in section 5.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW TO PLACE THE TWO PHONES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PHONE A  --  "MOUTH MIC"  (this stands in for the mic inside the helmet)
    10-15 cm from the speaker's mouth, microphone end pointing at the mouth.
    Slightly to the side of the mouth, not directly in front of it, so breath
    does not thump the microphone.

PHONE B  --  "NOISE MIC"  (this stands in for the mic on top of the helmet)
    60-100 cm away from the speaker's head, raised ABOVE head height if you can
    manage it, and with its microphone pointing AWAY from the speaker and
    TOWARDS the noise source.
    The idea: phone B should hear mostly noise and as little of the voice as
    possible. It does not need to be zero voice -- just clearly less.

    ASCII sketch, seen from above:

        [noise source / speaker playing gunfire]
                     |
                     |   about 2-3 m
                     v
              [PHONE B]  <-- mic pointing up/away from the person
                 ^
                 |  60-100 cm
                 |
            ( person )  --10-15cm--  [PHONE A]  <-- mic pointing at mouth

Write down the two distances you actually used. Approximate is fine ("about
70 cm"), but we need to know, and it must stay the same within one take.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE EXACT PROCEDURE FOR ONE TAKE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

   1. Both phones in position, Bluetooth off, voice recorder app open.
   2. Start recording on PHONE B first, then on PHONE A. (Order does not
      matter much, but both must be running before the clap.)
   3. Wait 3 seconds of silence.
   4. ONE SHARP CLAP, hands held roughly between the two phones.
   5. Wait 2 seconds.
   6. Say out loud: the take name, e.g. "Take 3, engine noise, Priya".
      This one spoken label saves us hours of guessing later.
   7. Start the noise (if this take has noise). Let it run 5 seconds BEFORE
      anyone speaks -- we need a stretch of noise with no voice in it.
   8. Read the script in section 8, at a normal unhurried pace.
   9. Stop speaking. Let the noise keep running for another 5 seconds.
  10. Stop the noise.
  11. Wait 2 seconds of silence.
  12. ONE SHARP CLAP again.
  13. Wait 3 seconds, then stop recording on BOTH phones.

The 5 seconds of noise-with-no-voice at each end (steps 7 and 9) are not
padding. The method needs a stretch of pure noise to lock onto, and we need it
to measure how much noise was removed. A take without them is much less useful.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHICH TAKES WE NEED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MUST HAVE -- please get all five of these. Each one is ONE take as described
above, recorded on BOTH phones simultaneously.

  TAKE 1   Quiet room, no noise at all.
           (This is the reference. Everything is compared against it.)

  TAKE 2   Recorded gunfire/battle audio from a speaker, LOUD.
           Play any gunfire or battle clip from YouTube through a speaker placed
           2-3 m away. Loud enough that you would struggle to hear the speaker
           clearly -- that is the realistic case.
           *** DO NOT use real firearms, firecrackers, or anything explosive.
               Played-back audio is exactly what we want. ***

  TAKE 3   Engine noise. A car or motorbike idling nearby, a generator, or a
           diesel pump. Outdoors is fine.

  TAKE 4   Crowd / babble. A canteen, market, or busy corridor at a busy time.
           No speaker needed -- the crowd IS the noise.

  TAKE 5   CALIBRATION TAKE, and please do not skip it even though it looks
           pointless: put BOTH PHONES SIDE BY SIDE, touching, 10-15 cm from the
           mouth. Clap, say "calibration", read only PART 2 of the script
           (the alphabet), clap, stop.
           This lets us measure how differently the two phones hear the same
           sound, and how far their clocks drift. Without it we cannot tell a
           real difference between the mics from a difference between the phones.

NICE TO HAVE -- if you have the time and energy:

  TAKE 6   Same as Take 2 (gunfire) but with the speaker much closer / louder,
           so the noise is clearly louder than the voice.
  TAKE 7   Wind: outdoors on a breezy day, or a fan 1 m away pointed at the mics.
  TAKE 8   A different speaker. Two or three different voices across the session
           is much better than one -- male and female, different accents.
  TAKE 9   ONE take in Hindi or your own first language, any noise condition.
           Read anything: a news paragraph, or just talk for 45 seconds.
           This one matters more than it looks: every recording our model has
           ever been trained or tested on is American-accented English, and our
           actual users will not sound like that.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. RECORDING SETTINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * Format: WAV / PCM if the app offers it. If it only offers m4a or mp3, that
    is acceptable -- do not let this stop you.
  * Sample rate: 48000 Hz if you can choose. 44100 is fine too. Use the SAME
    setting on both phones.
  * Mono is fine. Stereo is fine.
  * Turn OFF anything called: noise reduction, noise cancellation, voice
    enhancement, automatic gain, AGC, "clear voice", "meeting mode".
    These are usually off by default in a plain recorder app. If your app has
    them, find them and switch them off. They change the recording in ways we
    cannot undo.
  * Do not wear or use earphones during recording.
  * Aeroplane mode is a good idea (stops a call or notification ruining a take),
    but turn Bluetooth off separately -- aeroplane mode does not always do it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. THE SCRIPT TO READ  (same in every take)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Speak in a normal voice. Do not shout, do not whisper. Unhurried, with a short
pause between parts. Roughly 45 seconds in total.

Read it EXACTLY as written, including the parts that feel silly. Our scoring
tool counts specific known words, so changing them breaks the measurement.

  PART 1 -- sibilants and stops

    She sells sea shells by the sea shore.
    The fifth ship shifted six sacks of steel.
    Peter checked the thick stack of plates twice.
    This is a test of the speech transmission system.

  PART 2 -- phonetic alphabet

    Alpha. Bravo. Charlie. Delta. Echo. Foxtrot.
    Sierra. Tango. Victor. Whiskey. X-ray. Zulu.

  PART 3 -- digits

    One, two, three, four, five,
    six, seven, eight, nine, zero.

  PART 4 -- tactical phrases

    Command, this is Bravo Two, radio check, over.
    Position secured at grid four seven two nine.
    Requesting immediate support at checkpoint six.
    Say again your last, you are breaking up, over.
    Message received and understood. Out.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. NAMING THE FILES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

After each take, rename both files immediately. It is nearly impossible to sort
this out later from timestamps alone.

    take<N>_<condition>_<speaker>_A_mouth.wav
    take<N>_<condition>_<speaker>_B_noise.wav

Examples:

    take1_quiet_priya_A_mouth.wav
    take1_quiet_priya_B_noise.wav
    take2_gunfire_priya_A_mouth.wav
    take2_gunfire_priya_B_noise.wav
    take5_calibration_priya_A_mouth.wav
    take5_calibration_priya_B_noise.wav

The A/B part is the important bit: we must know which file came from the mouth
mic and which from the noise mic. If you are unsure afterwards, say so rather
than guessing -- a swapped pair inverts the whole thing.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Copy the block below into a text file or the body of your message, once per
session, and fill in the blanks. Two minutes of writing here saves us a lot of
uncertainty.

    Date:
    Place (city / type of room):
    Recorded by:

    Phone A (mouth mic): make and model ................ app used ............
    Phone B (noise mic): make and model ................ app used ............
    Format / sample rate used:

    Distance, mouth to phone A:            about ...... cm
    Distance, speaker's head to phone B:    about ...... cm
    Was phone B above head height?         yes / no
    Distance from noise source to phone B: about ...... m

    Takes recorded (tick and note the noise):
      [ ] Take 1  quiet
      [ ] Take 2  gunfire from speaker -- what clip / how loud:
      [ ] Take 3  engine -- what engine:
      [ ] Take 4  crowd -- where:
      [ ] Take 5  CALIBRATION, both phones side by side
      [ ] Take 6  gunfire, louder
      [ ] Take 7  wind
      [ ] Take 8  second speaker -- name:
      [ ] Take 9  Hindi / other language -- which:

    Speaker(s): name, male/female, first language, approximate age

    Anything that went wrong, was unusual, or you are unsure about:
    (a phone was nudged, a notification beeped, someone walked through,
     you forgot a clap, the noise stopped early -- ALL of this is useful to
     know and none of it means we discard the take. Just tell us.)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. HOW TO SEND THE FILES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * Google Drive, or any file-transfer link. Share the folder.
  * DO NOT send the audio through WhatsApp, Telegram or Instagram as a normal
    message or voice note. They re-compress audio aggressively and strip out
    exactly the detail we are measuring. If you must use WhatsApp, attach the
    files as a DOCUMENT, not as audio.
  * Send the ORIGINAL files. Do not trim them, do not edit them, do not run any
    "enhance audio" or "remove noise" tool, do not normalise the volume, and do
    not convert them to another format. We need them exactly as the phone wrote
    them, including the silences and the claps.
  * If a take went badly, send it anyway and say what happened. A flawed take we
    know about is more useful than a gap.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. FINAL CHECKLIST  (run through this before the first take)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  [ ] Bluetooth OFF on both phones, no earbuds anywhere near
  [ ] Plain voice recorder app open on both -- NOT the camera
  [ ] Noise reduction / voice enhancement / AGC off if the app has them
  [ ] Both phones propped up so they cannot move
  [ ] Phone A 10-15 cm from mouth, slightly to the side
  [ ] Phone B 60-100 cm away, pointing away from the speaker
  [ ] Both phones have enough battery and storage for ~15 minutes of audio
  [ ] You know where the clap goes: start AND end of every take
  [ ] The script (section 8) is open and readable from where the speaker stands
  [ ] You will say the take name out loud at the start of each take


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. WHAT HAPPENS TO THESE FILES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We align the two channels using your claps, measure how much of the voice leaks
into the noise mic, and then test whether having the noise mic lets the model
remove far more noise than it can from one microphone alone. If it does, that is
the route to the 15 dB target the problem statement sets, and it becomes a
headline result. If it does not, we will know that from measurement rather than
from hoping, which is worth almost as much.

One session with all five MUST-HAVE takes is already enough for us to start.
Two sessions in different places, with different speakers, is much better.

Thank you -- this is the one piece of the project that cannot be done in
software, and nobody can do it except the people with the microphones.
